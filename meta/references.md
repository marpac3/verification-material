# References — citation ID to bibliographic entry

Authoritative map from `[cit:ID]` to a full bibliographic string, consumed by the book
build to render numbered per-chapter References sections. IEEE-like style:
`Authors, "Title," Venue, Year.`

**Format contract:** one entry per line, `ID | bibliographic string`. The pipe `|` is the
field delimiter and never appears inside a bibliographic string. IDs are those of
`corpus_index.md`; every ID cited anywhere in `book/ch*.md` has exactly one entry here.
Sorted by ID group (B, D, P, R, S, T) then numerically.

**Parse rule:** an entry line matches `^[BDPRST][0-9]+ \| ` — anchor on that regex, not on
a loose split, because the provenance table at the end of this file also uses pipes.

**Provenance rule:** every author, title, venue and year below was read from the actual
PDF. `[n.d.]` means the year is **not printed anywhere in the source document** — the PDF's
embedded creation timestamp is recorded in "Provenance notes" instead, so the coordinator
can resolve it against the proceedings archive without re-opening the file. A year written
without `[n.d.]` was either printed in the document, or — for the D-group entries so noted
in "Provenance notes" — resolved against the DVCon proceedings archive at
dvcon-proceedings.org, with the source document URL recorded there.

Two further exceptions exist, both documented per entry in "Provenance notes" and nowhere
else. First, a **venue** may come from outside the document when the document prints none:
the DVCon edition for every entry resolved against the proceedings archive, the AITest
proceedings for P5 (from its CrossRef record, cited because the preprint's title and author
list match that record exactly), and the TOOLS 38 proceedings for P18 (from the publisher's
embedded PDF Title metadata, confirmed against CrossRef). P9 and P13 are partial cases of
the same kind: the venue IS printed — in P9's running footer on every page, and in the
banner P13 carries above its title — but the event **location** is not, and that comes from
the CrossRef record's `event` field. Second, **page ranges**
appear only where a registry supplied them (P5, P9, P13, P18); no page range was read off a
document. Every DOI
named in "Provenance notes" was resolved against `api.crossref.org/works/<doi>` and matched
on title and authors before being recorded; no DOI or ISBN is written into an entry line.
Apart from these, every author, title, venue and year below was read from the actual PDF.

## Provenance notes

DVCon proceedings carry the conference edition (US / Europe / India / China) and the year
at the volume level, not on the paper itself, so for most D-group entries neither appeared
anywhere in the document. Thirty such entries (D1, D2, D3, D5, D6, D9, D10, D11, D12, D13,
D14, D15, D16, D17, D18, D19, D20, D22, D23, D24, D26, D27, D29, D30, D31, D32, D33, D34,
D35, D36) have since been
resolved against the DVCon proceedings archive (dvcon-proceedings.org) — see the per-entry
source URL in
the table below. D28 sits beside that list rather than in it: it prints its own year, twice,
and only the US/Europe/India qualifier it omits came from the archive.
**D25 is neither in that list nor beside it: it is unresolved.** Added 2026-08-27 for ch25,
it is cited as "Proc. DVCon, 2025" with no edition, because the artifact names none. The
only in-document signal is the authors' Indian affiliation, which is weak evidence — this
file already records one case where a conference name inside a PDF turned out to be the
document template's lineage rather than the venue. The year is from the file's CreationDate
(2025-01-15). Resolve against the archive before citing the edition anywhere.

**B5 page locations, recorded so they are not re-derived.** The orthogonal-checking-mechanism
assumption lives at **printed p. 35, footnote 3**, in the author's own first person: "I assume
here that an orthogonal mechanism is in place to detect device misbehavior such as data and
temporal checkers." Its **p. 120** decoupling sentence is about *stimulus generation*, not
coverage — misreading it put a wrong claim into two chapters in opposite directions — and
**pp. 121–122** make checking and coverage explicitly *dependent* (shared input and output
monitors; the fidelity trade-off "is a function of the observability into the device").
Two working notes: this PDF has **no text layer past the front matter**, so pages must be
read as images; and its back matter is a **word concordance**, which makes "does this source
state this at all" answerable by intersecting three content words across their page lists —
the cheapest instrument in the corpus, and unused for most of this project. The PDF-to-printed
page offset is **not constant** (+12 in one chapter, +10 in another, +9 in the index):
recompute it from a page footer in each region rather than carrying it across.

**S6 is held but never cited, and that is deliberate.** A census across all 26 chapters and
both appendices returns zero markers for the VHDL language reference — it is the only entry
here in that position. The corpus holds it, the book works in SystemVerilog throughout, and
VHDL is mentioned twice in twenty-six chapters. The entry stays so that the choice is on
record as a choice: a reader who wonders whether VHDL was overlooked can see that it was
not. Note the direction of this file's own promise — *every cited ID has an entry* is true;
*every entry is cited* was never claimed and is now false by exactly one.

**P22 and P11: the arXiv identifier disagrees with the printed stamp, and the stamp wins.**
Both were challenged and both survived — recorded here so the challenge is not repeated.
P22's identifier begins `2510`, which reads as October 2025, while the stamp printed on
page 1 says **16 Sep 2025**; the entry follows the stamp. P11's identifier begins `2402`
(v1, February 2024) while the corpus file is **v4, stamped 25 Feb 2026** — the same month
two years later, which is precisely the coincidence that invites a misreading. The entry
cites v4 because v4 is the artifact in hand. **The identifier encodes the announcement of
one version, not the date of the version you are holding**, so it can neither date a paper
nor refute a date — the "never date from an arXiv ID" rule runs in both directions.
Consequence for anyone citing P11: a datum present in v4 may have originated in v1 two
years earlier, so check which version carries the number before attaching a year to it.

**P23's year is its arXiv v2 stamp.** The newest year printed anywhere in its own
bibliography is 2025, and the only 2026 in the document is the stamp. Because it is a v2,
the stamp and the `2512` identifier prefix are separate events, so this is *not* the
single-export corroboration trap recorded below — but it rests on one signal, and no claim
drawn from it in ch25 or ch26 depends on the year.

A recorded year is not automatically a resolved one: D16 stood at 2022 on
the strength of a date printed in every slide footer, which turned out to be a PowerPoint
auto-date field re-rendered during Accellera's 2022-03-02 bulk re-export — the archive
puts it at 2015. When an entry records WHY a field was believed, that stated basis is
itself auditable, and sweeping the corpus for a shared creation timestamp is what exposed
it. One further
DVCon paper carries a P-group ID because it is filed under `papers/` in the corpus (P7); it
was resolved by the same method and has a row of its own. The PDF creation timestamp is
retained in the table as corroborating evidence, not as the source of the printed value.
Any D-group entry still carrying `[n.d.]` remains genuinely unresolved; the PDF creation
timestamp for those is a bound on the publication date, not the date itself.

Note on direction of error when resolving: DVCon US camera-ready copy is prepared in
Dec/Jan for a late-Feb/early-Mar conference, so a PDF created in Dec of year N most likely
belongs to the year N+1 proceedings. This pattern was confirmed for D11 (created 2016-12-16
→ 2017 proceedings), D15 (created 2018-12-10 → 2019 proceedings) and D35 (created
2022-11-19 → 2023 proceedings).

Stronger than a timestamp match: for the six DVCon-family entries resolved in this pass
(D5, D23, D29, D33, D35, P7) the corpus PDF was checksummed (MD5) against the file the
archive serves from the resolved `/document/` page, and all six are byte-identical. That
removes the paper-vs-presentation title ambiguity that had to be argued from author lists
for D6 and D9, and it is what settles D29 — see its row. A later pass added D13 and D31 by
the same method: both corpus PDFs are byte-identical to the file the archive serves from the
resolved `/document/` page, with the MD5 recorded in each row below, and for both, the `-2`
duplicate slug the archive would mint for a second entry under the same title returns 404, so
neither has a paper-vs-presentation alternative to argue. A third pass added D14, D18, D34
and D36 the same way; all four are byte-identical to the archive-served file, with the MD5
in each row. D36 was the first case where the sibling slugs matter rather than being ruled
out (D2, in the fifth pass below, is the second and the more dangerous): D36
prints no title at all, and its `-3` sibling is the DVCon U.S. 2026 edition of a
deck the archive also holds for Europe 2024 and India 2025 — the last of which is the
corpus file D8. A fourth pass added D3, D17 and D24 the same way; all three corpus PDFs are
byte-identical to the archive-served file, with the MD5 in each row, and for D3 and D17 the
`-2` sibling slug returns 404. D24 was the first entry found whose `/document/` slug is
NOT the stem of its PDF filename — the file is served as
`…-industrial-results-presentation.pdf` but the entry resolves at
`…-industrial-results/`, and the `-presentation` slug 404s. Deriving the slug from the
filename is therefore a heuristic, not a rule; when it 404s, the uploads path still
identifies the file and the entry has to be found from there.

A fifth pass added the remaining seven D-group entries — D2, D12, D19, D27, D28, D30 and
D32 — by the same method; all seven corpus PDFs are byte-identical to the archive-served
file, with the MD5 recorded in each row, and for all but D2 the `-2` sibling slug returns
404. That pass turned the slug heuristic from an exception into a class, and it strengthens
the rule rather than merely adding cases. D24 is no longer alone: D28 and D32 need the same
`-presentation` qualifier dropped before their entries resolve, so three entries now resolve
at a slug the filename does not give by that same mechanism. D30 fails differently — its
filename has lost a space
(`the-how-tosof-…`), and the derived slug does not 404 but 301-redirects to
`the-how-tos-of-…`, so the redirect has to be followed rather than a qualifier trimmed.
D2 fails worse than either. Its filename-derived slug returns 200, at a real entry with the
right title, the right edition and the right year — which is nevertheless the wrong record.
The archive holds a DVCon Europe 2022 Paper and a DVCon Europe 2022 Presentation under this
title; the corpus file is the Presentation, which lives at the `-2` slug, while the Paper
entry serves a different file altogether (a 1-page `79546.pdf`). So byte-identity to a file
under `wp-content/uploads/` proves which FILE is held, and only the candidate entry's own
Download href proves which ENTRY serves it. A 200 is not a match, and the two checks are
separate.

D12 is that pass's other correction, and it concerns metadata rather than slugs. Its PDF
prints `Author: DVCon Europe` beside `CreationDate: 2019-08-05`. Reading the Author field as
an editorial venue statement and the timestamp as a year yields "DVCon Europe, 2019"; the
archive, against a byte-identical file, records DVCon India 2019. The Author field is the
Word template's, inherited by papers written for any edition, which makes a conference name
in PDF metadata template lineage of the same class as the Sarnoff-Symposium Title string on
D10, D11, D15 and D27 — corroborating at best, never a venue. Half of a container property
is still a container property. Contrast D17, where the metadata string `DVCON2010_108-PY527`
IS editorial: a submission ID with a submission number is specific to one paper and one
conference, where a bare organisation name is specific to neither.

Creation timestamps are NOT reliable for archive-hosted decks. D29 is the counterexample:
its PDF was created 2022-03-02 but the document is from the 2019 proceedings, because
Accellera re-exported decks when it rebuilt the proceedings archive. Where an archive year
and a creation timestamp disagree, the archive year governs and the timestamp is discarded.
D13 makes this a pattern rather than a single counterexample: it carries the identical
2022-03-02 creation date and is a 2018 document, so both decks were re-exported the same day.

Preprints: this pass introduced the first arXiv-sourced entries in the file (P8, P11, P12,
P25). They use the shape `arXiv preprint arXiv:<id>v<n>, <Month Year>`, naming the exact
version read, because an arXiv ID identifies a record with several versions and its numeric
prefix is the month of the FIRST upload, not the date of the version in hand — for P11 the
gap is two years. Where a preprint's title and author list match a published record exactly,
the published venue is cited instead of the preprint (P5, and P13 in the fifth pass). Where
they do not match, the
preprint is cited and the related publication is recorded in the row below (P11, P25).

P14 was added in a later pass and follows the same shape. Its stamp date is the one printed
on the page, and the ID prefix is deliberately NOT offered as corroboration of it: for a v1
the stamp and the ID derive from a single upload event and agree by construction, so citing
one to support the other is the "two container properties agreeing" failure that produced
the D16 error. The independent signal is inside the document — see its row. P13, added in
the fifth pass, is the second entry to refuse the ID-prefix corroboration explicitly — P8's
and P12's rows do not discuss the prefix at all — and it is the better-placed of the two,
because it carries a printed IEEE copyright block and so does not need the arXiv stamp for
its year at all.

P4 and P15 were added in the industrial-anchoring pass, after both were found to be cited in
