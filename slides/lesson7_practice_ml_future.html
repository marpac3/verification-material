    An artifact owner is not the whole outcome

    Integration seams need explicit responsibility.

    • Name who resolves cross-team discrepancies.
    • Keep the expected evidence visible at handoff.
    HARDWARE VERIFICATION LESSON 74

    Use a checker and model owned by different teams. Both can be delivered while their disagreement remains unresolved.

    Assign responsibility for the outcome, including the interface between artifacts. The owner should be able to say which evidence permits the receiving team to proceed.

    Engineering practice and automation

    Reviews answer different questions

    Plan, code and closure reviews need different inputs.

    Intent review→Implementation review→Closure review
      HARDWARE VERIFICATION LESSON 75

      Ask which review detects a missing requirement, which challenges a checker implementation and which accepts residual risk. Treating them as one generic approval loses those distinctions.

      Bring the artifact that makes each question answerable. A coverage dashboard does not replace the specification at a plan review, and a code review does not authorize a waiver.

      Engineering practice and automation

      Reproduction is an onboarding milestone

      A newcomer should establish evidence before explaining architecture.

      • Start from a known failure and its reproducer.
      • Produce a useful bug report and trace the ownership path.
      HARDWARE VERIFICATION LESSON 76

      A guided defect teaches the actual run environment, observations and team interfaces. Ask the newcomer to reproduce it without depending on an undocumented working directory.

      Judge onboarding by a small useful artifact rather than vocabulary recall. The exercise also exposes missing process documentation in the team.

      Discussion: every deliverable has an owner

      Name the evidence that would settle the decision.

      • The model and checker were both delivered.
      • Their disagreement blocks integration.
      • Which responsibility was left unnamed?
      HARDWARE VERIFICATION LESSON 77

      Expected answer: responsibility for resolving the combined verification outcome at the seam, including authority to settle intent.

      Ask learners to define the evidence and escalation path that make the handoff complete. Merely adding another status meeting does not resolve ownership.

      Engineering practice and automation

      Evaluate the proposed operational use

      A measured task outcome is more useful than generic accuracy.

      • Use held-back data and a relevant comparison.
      • Re-measure when the design or failure population changes.
      HARDWARE VERIFICATION LESSON 78

      Use the chapter’s triage-classifier proposal. Agreement with the human triage decisions measures the task the classifier is being proposed to assist, within that held-back population.

      A changed design or failure distribution can invalidate the estimate. Keep an owner, monitoring cadence and retraining cost with the deployment decision.

      Engineering practice and automation

      A benchmark needs its denominator

      Artifact class and acceptance criterion determine the meaning.

      • Record filtering and excluded cases.
      • Compare like tasks before comparing headline results.
      HARDWARE VERIFICATION LESSON 79

      The chapter compares studies producing different artifacts under different criteria. Ask what was counted as successful, what humans filtered, and what target set remained.

      A result on one protocol or design does not establish a trend across workflows. Describe the reported experiment precisely before drawing a transfer claim for the local project.

      Engineering practice and automation

      A generated checker can become a bad oracle

      A proof against current RTL does not establish intended behavior.

      • Inspect timing direction and activation.
      • Challenge both design and generated interpretation.
      HARDWARE VERIFICATION LESSON 710

      A syntactically valid property may express the wrong temporal relation or never activate. A proof against the current design can confirm consistency while preserving the original intent error.

      Ask what independent observation would reject the plausible but wrong property. Mechanical checking reduces the candidate set; it does not remove the need to inspect the intended claim.

      Discussion: most generated properties prove

      Name the evidence that would settle the decision.

      • Generated properties pass against the current RTL.
      • No one has checked them against intent.
      • What has the reported result established?
      HARDWARE VERIFICATION LESSON 711

      Expected answer: consistency with that design under the proof environment, for the properties and candidates included in the measurement.

      It has not established that the properties express the intended behavior or cover all required plan rows. Ask which acceptance work is still owed.

      Engineering practice and automation

      Filter mechanically before intent review

      Human attention should reach candidates with a clear provenance.

      Mechanical checks→Intent review→Recorded admission
        HARDWARE VERIFICATION LESSON 712

        Use parsing, elaboration, signal resolution and available semantic checks to remove mechanical failures before asking a reviewer to assess intent.

        The reviewer still decides whether the surviving property expresses the requirement and is admitted into the plan. A tool pass must not silently perform that admission.

        Engineering practice and automation

        Correlated errors defeat casual sampling

        A shared generation mistake can affect an entire batch.

        • Inspect systematic translation risks.
        • Do not infer batch correctness from a few plausible examples.
        HARDWARE VERIFICATION LESSON 713

        Explain why repeated use of the same flawed template or interpretation creates dependent errors. A small informal sample does not establish the reliability of the unreviewed remainder.

        Choose review and validation according to the artifact’s role as an oracle. The chapter keeps acceptance authority with the people responsible for the verification claim.

        Engineering practice and automation

        Preserve the accepted artifact, not just the model name

        Provenance must support reconstruction of the decision.

        • Retain relevant inputs, generated text and accepted revision.
        • Record reviewer, rationale and applicable restrictions.
        HARDWARE VERIFICATION LESSON 714

        A model identifier does not identify the prompt context, retrieved specification, generation or human edits that produced the checker in use.

        Keep the actual accepted artifact and the evidence supporting admission. Apply the project’s data-handling rules to the inputs and outputs before using the workflow.

        Engineering practice and automation

        Discussion: approval of a sampled batch

        Name the evidence that would settle the decision.

        • A reviewer checks a few generated properties.
        • All share the same translation template.
        • What uncertainty remains about the batch?
        HARDWARE VERIFICATION LESSON 715

        Expected answer: a systematic error in the shared template or interpretation can affect many unreviewed properties. The sample’s plausibility does not establish their intent correctness.

        Ask for a validation action targeted at that shared failure mode and a traceable acceptance decision for the artifacts actually admitted.

        Engineering practice and automation

        The bottleneck depends on the project level

        A block and a software workload can need different improvements.

        • Separate knowing what to check from execution capacity.
        • Measure the local constraint before investing.
        HARDWARE VERIFICATION LESSON 716

        Contrast a block regression that already fits overnight with a system workload that cannot reach its useful software state in time. More execution capacity addresses only one of those immediate constraints.

        The chapter’s claim is conditional on level and workload. Do not turn it into a universal prediction about every verification team.

        Engineering practice and automation

        Earlier and later evidence are complementary

        Moving a check does not erase its original scope obligations.

        • Use early models with explicit fidelity limits.
        • Retain deep block checking alongside system execution.
        HARDWARE VERIFICATION LESSON 717

        Earlier models expose integration or software problems before RTL is ready; later platforms exercise behaviors that need long execution or physical observations.

        Neither movement justifies dropping a plan row without replacement evidence for the same claim. Review the handoff of model assumptions and residual behavior.

        Engineering practice and automation

        Automation moves work without removing judgment

        Partial oracles and model boundaries remain engineering problems.

        • Improve the weakest evidence-producing step.
        • Keep interpretation, review and ownership explicit.
        HARDWARE VERIFICATION LESSON 718

        Separate contingent tool capacity from durable limitations such as incomplete specifications and partial observation. Better automation can change the cost of a task without deciding what correctness should mean.

        Ask learners to name a proposed improvement and the claim it strengthens. This keeps future-looking discussion tied to an inspectable engineering outcome.

        Engineering practice and automation

        Discussion: cut regression after a new proof

        Name the evidence that would settle the decision.

        • A new formal result motivates removing simulation work.
        • Its model covers only part of the old claim.
        • What evidence is required before the cut?
        HARDWARE VERIFICATION LESSON 719

        Expected answer: show that the retained and replacement evidence jointly discharge the original plan row, including behavior outside the proof model.

        A proof’s strength does not make it transferable to an unrelated boundary. Preserve the residual simulation obligation or explicitly revise the claim with a reviewed risk decision.

        Exercise: compare two automation proposals

        Distinguish task evidence from design consistency.

        • One classifier is measured against held-back human triage.
        • Generated properties instead prove against current RTL.
        • Explain which result measures its proposed use and what remains unknown.
        HARDWARE VERIFICATION LESSON 720

        Exercise 25.2 (judgement). The chapter opens with two proposals reaching one verification lead in the same week. The first offers a failure classifier for the video codec's nightly regression, which agreed with the human triage engineer 88% of the time on a held-back month of history. The second offers a specification-to-assertion flow piloted on the crypto engine, most of whose generated properties passed a formal proof against the current RTL. Decide which of the two reports evidence about the use it proposes, and give the property of the first measurement that the second one lacks. State what the passing proofs do establish, pointing to the section that explains why a proof verdict settles consistency rather than intent.

        Assign book Exercise 25.2 in full. Use the chapter’s stated held-back comparison without treating its illustrative result as a prediction for another project.

        Expected answer: the classifier measurement evaluates the proposed operational task against independent human triage on held-back history. Passing proofs show consistency with the modeled RTL and assumptions; they do not establish that generated properties express the intended specification.
