A HOLISTIC GUIDE4

Chapter 24 · Where the boundaries fail

Everybody did their job. The outcome had no owner.

Week 41 of the reference SoC. A system-level test running a quantized network on the neural accelerator, with both DMA channels streaming, produces one wrong output tensor in roughly four hundred inferences. The mechanism: the accelerator's weight prefetcher wraps its buffer pointer one entry early when the weight stream ends mid-beat under DMA contention. It reproduces only at 2-bit weight precision, only with a degenerate tensor geometry, and only when the other channel is moving traffic. The block signed off in week 33 at 100% statement and branch coverage.

The post-mortem, and why nobody is wrong in it The accelerator's verifier: memory contention is a system-level concern — her environment drove the accelerator's ports directly, as the plan said, and she closed every quantization row. The SoC integration verifier: quantization corners are a block-level concern — his job was connectivity and system traffic, with no visibility into weight-buffer geometry. The DMA verifier was not invited, because the DMA was not at fault. Every artifact in the project had a named owner. The outcome the escape falsified — is the accelerator demonstrably correct while something else is competing for memory? — had none.

SayNobody in that room can be told to do better next time, which is exactly the diagnosis. This is an ownership defect, not a diligence defect, and it is invisible until an escape reveals it.

A HOLISTIC GUIDE5

Chapter 24 · Roles

What is a role answerable for?

Ownership by artifact — the default “She owns the block environment. He owns the SoC environment. That one owns the coverage model.” Administratively convenient, maps cleanly onto repository directories, and arrives without anyone deciding it. Discharged by the artifact being good.
Ownership by outcome Not “owns the accelerator testbench” but “owns whether the accelerator is demonstrably correct under every condition the system can present to it”. Discharged only by evidence about the design.

This is not a rhetorical upgrade of the same sentence. The two formulations disagree about who is at fault when the environment is excellent and the answer is still unknown. The book's vocabulary already contains the move one level down: an RTL designer's responsibility is not to write RTL — that is a means to an end — but to produce a working design, which is why the whole design team, not the verification team alone, contributes to the plan and answers for its completeness. Applied to verification roles, “my rows are closed” stops being a defence and becomes an admission: closed rows are an artifact statement.

AUTHOR'S READ  The test that separates them Ask an owner to state what would have to be true for their answer to be wrong. An artifact owner describes a bug in the artifact; an outcome owner describes a condition of the design they have not yet reached. A project that cannot get the second answer for a feature does not have an owner for it — it has a directory.

SayThe chapter flags that test as its own judgment, not a published rule — so teach it as a habit you are recommending, not as a finding. And hold on to its shape: slide 44 asks the same question about a whole sign-off.

Chapter 24 · Roles

Boundaries do not fail in the middle of a block. They fail at three seams.

SeamWhat actually goes wrongWhat closes it
Block → systemAt integration each block generally arrives from its own team, and the SoC verification team must debug and analyse the assembled system without knowledge of the IP internals — each block is a black box to that team, which has neither the time nor the background to understand the inner workings of several complex IPs at once. Structural, not a staffing failure.Name an owner for each cross-block outcome — “the accelerator under memory contention”, “the flagship's DMA under a coherent writeback” — and put that owner on the block side, where the internals are understood, obliged to state what system conditions the block environment did not present.
Design → verificationNot a wall at all. Design engineers spent, on average, 49% of their time on verification activities in 2024 — half a designer's working life is already inside this discipline.Stop drawing it as a hand-over. A boundary drawn that way produces the familiar pathology: design-side checks written but never regressed, because they belong to no suite.
Feature → nobodyRows whose evidence is expected from somewhere else — a protocol checked by purchased verification IP, a corner covered by the emulation run, a mode “the software team exercises”.An assumption is a row whose owner is a sentence. Give it a person or delete it.

SayThe first row is the one people argue with. It is not a criticism of integration engineers — it is a fact about what any integration team can know, which is why the fix moves the owner rather than the workload.

The 49% in row 2: 2024 Wilson Research Group IC/ASIC functional verification trend report, commissioned by Siemens EDA — [R2, p. 7]. Double-blind study, ±4% at 95% confidence.

A HOLISTIC GUIDE7

Chapter 24 · Roles

Adopting an artifact can transfer an outcome nobody re-assigned

Scenario · the video codec's reference C model The codec's checking rests on a bit-exact C model. The design team wrote it first, to explore the entropy-coding trade-offs, and it is excellent — faster and better tested than anything the verification team would have produced in the time. So verification adopts it as its checking reference and compares the RTL against it frame by frame. Ownership by artifact says this is efficient: design owns the model, verification owns the comparison, nobody duplicates work.
Approach Keep the model, but be precise about what it is — a transfer function written by the design team, not a golden model — and move the outcome, “the codec's output is what the standard requires”, to the verification side. Discharge it with evidence the C model cannot produce: conformance streams from the standards body decoded against both, an independent reader written from the specification for the syntax layer, and a written record of every place the C model was tuned to match the RTL rather than the specification.
Why A model written by the design team encodes the design team's reading of the specification, so it is not the independent second interpretation a reference model has to be. Comparing RTL against it reconverges on that reading, and the comparison can stay green on a misread clause forever. The artifact is fine. What was wrong is that adopting it silently transferred an outcome nobody re-assigned.

SayNote the shape of the fix: nothing is thrown away and nobody is blamed. The change is one sentence about who answers for a claim — which is what makes it cheap enough to actually do.

A HOLISTIC GUIDE9

Chapter 24 · Reviews

Reviews fail politely far more often than they fail rudely

AUTHOR'S READ  Three of the four mechanics below are the chapter's own judgment, offered because they are cheap and their absence is diagnostic. Only the fourth is drawn from the corpus.

  • Circulate the artifact before the meeting, and open by asking whether people read it. A review where the author narrates the document aloud has already failed — narration is a presentation and the reviewers are an audience.
  • Someone chairs who is not the author, so the author can answer questions instead of managing the clock.
  • Every finding leaves as an action with a name and a date, closed before the next gate rather than at it.
  • Attach the review record to the object it judged. Records linked directly to plan objects make the history retrievable during audits and quality reviews — the difference between a review that accumulates into institutional memory and one that evaporates into a meeting-notes folder.
A fifth mechanic, about tone rather than procedure Demand clear justification of how each specification point is verified. Accept that the justification may be complex and needs thoughtful review. And do not tolerate, or encourage, hand-waving.
The failure that is invisible from inside the meeting Where a review has to span two levels of abstraction — a formal property set against a coverage model, say — the mismatch makes the review process much harder, a deep dive into individual items is often required, and the time for it has to be allowed for. A one-hour slot for something needing a half-day does not produce a bad judgement. It produces no judgement, delivered on time.

SayAnd the way every rite dies under schedule pressure is the same: the review happens after the decision it was meant to inform, so its findings can only be recorded as risks.

A HOLISTIC GUIDE10

Chapter 24 · Reviews

An assumption naming a team is not closed until someone from that team names the row

Scenario · the coherent hub's plan review The hub's own rows are dense and well argued — MESI state transitions, directory occupancy, the livelock and starvation cases. In the assumptions section sits one line: “IO-coherent port behaviour under snoop-in is covered by the accelerator teams.” Two of the three accelerator plans contain the mirror-image sentence: “coherency is handled by the hub.” Nobody wrote a row.
The counterparty rule An assumption naming a team is not closed until someone from that team has read it in this review and named the row that discharges it. Here that produces two new rows on the hub's plan — snoop-in during an in-flight accelerator write, and snoop-in to a line the accelerator has just released — and one row on each mirroring accelerator plan.

Why: both sentences are individually reasonable and jointly fatal. Unspoken and undocumented assumptions are how design features fall through the cracks — which is why every assumption is reviewed with the designers face to face, usually over several rounds before alignment.
The same discipline, one rite over — the crossbar's coverage-model review A proposed cross would stress ID handling at a subordinate port: two managers presenting the same manager-side ID with writes in flight to one subordinate. The designer kills it in a sentence. The interconnect appends source-identifying bits toward the subordinate, so the subordinate-side ID is 7 bits — the 4-bit manager ID plus the 3 bits needed to distinguish 5 manager ports — and the two managers arrive with different IDs. The cell can never be hit, by construction. The row is not deleted, it is replaced, and the minutes record why.

SayAn unreachable cover point does not fail loudly. It sits at zero, absorbs stimulus effort when time is shortest, and is eventually excluded by an engineer who no longer remembers whether it was impossible or merely unreached — while the hazard it represented stays unmodelled.

Chapter 24 · Maturity stages

Three conversations that change shape — from confidence to evidence

The questionAnswered without stagesAnswered with them
“Are you on track?”A percentage and a tone of voice, neither of which the asker can audit. A plan carrying too little detail communicates poorly with other teams, leaves them confused about what has actually been verified, costs the verification team their trust, and pushes everyone into proxies for progress — coverage percentages, test counts.A stage claim is auditable in an hour by someone who does not work on the block, because every line of a stage checklist is a demonstration, not an estimate. SayStages also make it socially possible to say not yet without saying we are behind — the stage is a state, not a verdict. Teams that cannot say “not yet” say “nearly” instead, for months.

A HOLISTIC GUIDE12

Chapter 24 · Maturity stages

A stage claimed rather than demonstrated has become a status word

The signature distinguishes stage inflation from ordinary optimism: the stage arrives without its checklist. And a status word can be assigned by whoever wants it assigned — which is generally not the team. The conversational countermeasure belongs to every engineer, not just to leads: never report a stage without the open lines. “Stage 2, three lines open” is a complete sentence. “Stage 2” is a slogan.

Scenario · the sensor hub arrives labelled “verification mature” Delivered by a partner team as a pre-verified block for the reference SoC's always-on island. The integration lead does not argue about the claim; he asks for the stage-2 checklist with its tick marks. It comes back with the stimulus, checker and regression lines ticked and three lines blank: retention behaviour across the hub's ultra-low-power modes, asynchronous wake events crossing into the system domain, and the functional coverage model for low-power mode transitions.
Approach Accept the block at stage 2 for the functions the checklist covers. The three open lines become rows on the SoC plan, with the partner team named as owner and a date. Integration proceeds; the low-power sequencing tests are gated on those rows, not on goodwill.
Why The claim “verification mature” was not false, it was unqualified. The checklist converted a trust question — do we believe this team? — into an evidence question both sides read the same way, and it did so without anyone having to allege anything. That is the whole social function of the vocabulary.

SayOne caution worth repeating in any status meeting: a stage describes the maturity of the verification, not the cleanliness of the design. A block at stage 2 with fifty open bugs is reporting that the machinery which finds bugs is working — which is exactly what stage 2 claims.

A HOLISTIC GUIDE13

Chapter 24 · Ramp-up

Verification engineers often arrive from design — and the reflexes come with them

What transfers, and it is a lot Reading RTL at speed, which decides how fast a failure gets localized. Micro-architectural intuition about where designs are fragile: which counters wrap, which FIFOs have a subtle almost-full, which handshakes have a reset hole. Knowing how the block was built. And standing with the design team when a verifier has to say a failure is a design bug.

The mindset is usually reached only through years of experience, mentoring and lessons learned the hard way. So the transition is slow — a two-day tool course is not a ramp-up — and it is taught, not read, so a wiki is not one either.

Intel: 85 engineers trained on formal property verification in two weeks, then four weeks of onsite consultancy — Making Formal Property Verification Mainstream: An Intel Graphics Experience, DVCon — [D18, p. 1]. Self-reported; a ramp onto one technique, not the mindset.

What works against them. Reflexes, not deficiencies — each correct in the previous job, and several make a testbench look healthier while it checks less.

Explaining before reproducing AUTHOR'S READA verifier's first obligation is a failure that replays on demand. Hypothesis-first gives a diagnosis with no artifact behind it.
Interoperating, not stressingThe more tolerant the testbench, the less it checks. Recognizable form: clock snooping. Fewer failing tests, faster bring-up, masked clocking bugs.
“Invalid” as a stimulus filterWhat matters is whether the scenario can occur — and if it can, the recovery from it is a feature.
A testbench that recoversA testbench is closer to a mousetrap: once it has caught something, effort spent keeping it going is effort in the wrong place.
Debugging in waveformsA verification component's work happens in zero time and in dynamic data structures. The log is where it gets debugged.
Staying zoomed inThe work requires switching deliberately between zoomed-in tasks and zoomed-out ones — where effort buys the most checking.

A HOLISTIC GUIDE15

Chapter 24 · Process

Process is the accumulated answer to “how do we not make that mistake again”

InstrumentWhat it actually buys — which is usually narrower than teams claim
TemplatesConsistency across authors. A plan template is called crucial because a large project has many plan authors feeding one guide. The real product is not the section headings: it is that ten plans can be read by one reviewer without ten context switches.
Coding standardsOne published hierarchy separates rules — which must be followed, because breaking them costs the productivity the methodology exists to provide — from recommendations, where the detail is often explicitly unimportant (a naming convention is the example given), but adherence within a team is strongly recommended. For a large class of standards the content is arbitrary and the agreement is everything.
Libraries and checklistsA lower entry barrier and a memory. Cookie-cutter property templates for the modules that recur — arbiters, state machines, standard interfaces — so a novice meets a filled-in starting point rather than a blank file.
A calendar slotStated flatly: unless the practice is integrated into mainstream deliverables, it will not be exercised despite its benefits — which is why check-points analogous to the existing milestones were introduced. A technique not on somebody's deliverable list does not happen.

SayWhat it costs is adherence and discipline, charged continuously rather than once. Wholesale adoption of a full methodology may simply be unaffordable for a real project with real people and schedules — an argument for adopting individual elements incrementally, not for adopting none. And the alternative is not free either: best-known methods simply not being in use is estimated to cost on the order of 20% of productivity on its own. AndOne coding standard genuinely has content: comments written for a reader who knows the language but not the design, and must maintain it without the original designer's help.

A HOLISTIC GUIDE16

Chapter 24 · The limits of process

The line is not good process versus bad. It is auditable claim versus laundered judgement.

It cannot decide whether its own line is true“Functional coverage model implemented and reporting in nightly regression” is objectively true or false; anyone can go and look. “Coverage is adequate for this feature” is a judgement wearing a tick-box. Every line of a gating checklist must name an artifact a skeptic could regenerate — and a line that cannot be written that way belongs in a review discussion instead.
It cannot see what is missing from the planEvery line audits a question somebody already thought to ask. This deck's opening escape would have passed a stage-3 checklist unblemished, because no line asked about the block under memory contention. Which is why escape analysis exists, and why its output is new rows.
It cannot make a review honestIt converts a judgement into a tick — useful for recall, useless against motive. If the room wants the gate passed, the checklist supplies a format for passing it.
It cannot narrow the spread that matters mostIndividual productivity across engineers ranges up to tenfold. Process compresses variance in the mechanical parts — how a bug is filed, when a regression runs — and does approximately nothing to which corner someone thinks to chase, or whether an odd waveform is investigated at 5 p.m. on a Friday.
The tell — a checklist line containing the word adequate, sufficient or acceptable Split it. The auditable half stays on the checklist; the judgement becomes a named review item with a threshold agreed in advance.

The tenfold spread: Oracle Labs, I created the Verification Gap, DVCon U.S. 2015 — [D16, slide 7]. Practitioners' own figure, not a controlled study.

SayThe worked case is the radar front-end's precision sign-off: an inherited line, “quantization error characterized against the reference model across the operating range”, ticked on every project since. The characterization ran and the report exists — and nobody asks whether that error is acceptable for the CFAR stage downstream, whose false-alarm rate is what the customer actually buys. AndThere is a point past which process stops paying, with a recognizable shape: too much detail too soon, where the result communicates worse, not better.

A HOLISTIC GUIDE18

Chapter 25 · How to read a number

Both numbers are real. Only one is evidence about the thing being proposed.

Proposal 1 · the video codec's triage classifier Trained on eighteen months of failure logs; it sorts each morning's failures into buckets and names a probable cause. On a held-back month of history it agreed with the human triage engineer 88% of the time.

Measured against a baseline the team owns: yesterday's triage decisions, made by a person, written down, and available to disagree with. It can be re-measured next quarter on next quarter's failures — and if agreement falls, the team will see it fall.
Proposal 2 · the crypto engine's assertion pilot A language model was given the engine's specification chapter and produced forty SystemVerilog assertions in about twenty minutes. Thirty-eight compile. Thirty-six pass a formal proof against the current RTL.

This measures agreement between a generated property and the design it was generated to check — precisely what an assertion must not be judged by. A property whose antecedent this design never satisfies passes without ever having been evaluated. Thirty-six passing proofs is a statement about compilation and consistency. It is not a statement about intent — and intent is the only thing a specification-derived property exists to capture.

The field's own reviewers say as much. A 2025 review of machine learning for micro-electronic design verification observes that although these techniques have been proposed since the late 1990s, many never reached mainstream adoption — and names three reasons that are about evidence rather than capability: withheld code and data make results hard to reproduce; techniques are evaluated on simple designs against random stimulus and little else; and research rarely asks whether a method generalises past the one application it was tried on.

SayTwo risks attend a subject like this — that it ages into embarrassment, and that an engineer promised a great deal and shown less reads it cynically and stops. Both are managed the same way: separate what has been demonstrated from what is claimed, and date everything. A vendor demonstration is not evidence.

A HOLISTIC GUIDE19

Chapter 25 · Classical machine learning

The least glamorous techniques here are the most deployed — and each optimises something narrower than the goal

Three applications have a genuine track record: steering stimulus toward uncovered behaviour, sorting regression failures, and choosing which tests to run. Their published results span two decades rather than two years. The value in reading them now is not the numbers — it is the shape of the numbers.

The founding coverage-directed result, 2003 A Bayesian network trained to direct stimulus at the storage control element of an IBM z-series system. The coverage model had five attributes with a cross product of 13,888 combinations, of which 1,968 were legal coverage tasks. Training used 160 test-cases, each taking more than thirty minutes to run, and covered 1,745 of them; 223 remained.

The trained network covered all 223 within 250 further test-cases — where an expert-written directive file covered two-thirds of them after more than 400.
Read the second half of that before the first The baseline was a human expert's tuned directive file, not uniform random stimulus. That is rare, and it is what makes the result mean something. The 2025 review notes that random is the most common baseline in this literature, and cautions that beating random says nothing about whether a technique generalises.
And the cost is in the same figures 160 training cases at more than thirty minutes each is more than eighty hours of simulation before the model produced its first useful directive — on top of building a coverage model precise enough to learn from.

SayThe structural point from Chapter 6 is what makes all of this behave the way it does: every such technique learns the mapping from generator directives to achieved coverage and inverts it, which means it optimises the derivative rather than the goal.

Cross product, legal tasks and both learning curves: Fine and Ziv, IBM Research Haifa, Coverage Directed Test Generation for Functional Verification using Bayesian Networks, DAC 2003 — [P16, pp. 5–6]. Self-reported. The per-test runtime, and the eighty-hour figure derived from it, are not among the figures checked on those pages.

The economics That survey also reports a typical machine-learning project spending up to 70% of the team's effort on preparing data. On the video codec's nightly that is not hypothetical: the failure population is dominated by golden-model mismatches whose useful label is a frame region and a coding tool — and neither is in the log unless somebody puts it there.
Which is why Chapter 12's discipline is what makes this affordable The triage engineer's confirmation is the training data — and only if that confirmation is recorded in a form a model can read. A rota that closes buckets in a chat thread has spent the labour and thrown away the label.
A HOLISTIC GUIDE22

Chapter 25 · Test selection

A compressed regression is a change-detection instrument, not a bug-finding one

A ranking model optimises Time-to-first-failure over a known suite — it reorders existing test-and-seed pairs so the runs most likely to fail go first.
A selection model optimises Coverage per simulation-second — discarding runs whose contribution it predicts is redundant.
Neither optimises The discovery of new behaviour. The industrial study behind the ranking figures says so in its own words: a ranked regression simulates no new scenarios and is not recommended for exploring the random space or exposing new bugs.

That is a constraint on where the technique can sit, not a caveat to file away. A ranked or compressed regression answers “did this commit break something the suite already knew how to catch?” quickly and cheaply. A program that replaces its full random regression with a compressed one has quietly converted its main source of new bugs into a fast regression check.

The honest deployment keeps both instruments A compressed suite per commit, and a full sweep with fresh seeds on a slower cadence — whose yield is tracked as a signal in its own right. The failure mode is quiet and arithmetic: regression runtime halves, and the bug discovery rate follows it down where nobody is plotting it.

SayThis is the one entry on the adopt-now list that comes with a mandatory companion. If a team takes the compression and drops the sweep, the technique has made the programme worse while every dashboard improved.

A HOLISTIC GUIDE23

Chapter 25 · The decay mode

Every technique on the last four slides shares one failure — and it is why published results do not survive contact with a programme

A model trained on one design's coverage response, one quarter's failure logs, or one release's regression outcomes is a claim that tomorrow resembles yesterday. The 2025 review names this directly: an example that was novel becomes unremarkable once more examples have been seen, so the model needs regular retraining to stay relevant — and worse, once deployed, the learner shapes the examples it will later be retrained on, which can suppress exploration and make performance decrease over time. The same review observes that research usually frames verification as a one-time learning problem while industrial designs change continuously, and warns that if substantial effort goes into crafting fitness or reward functions, the technique has merely shifted effort from writing tests to tuning models.

Consequence 1 · retraining is a task, not a maintenance detail It is named, owned, scheduled, and has a cost. A triage classifier 90% accurate at tape-out and 60% accurate two derivatives later is worse than none, because the rota has stopped checking it. Give it a measured agreement rate, re-measure on a fixed cadence against human decisions, and publish the number where the people relying on it can see it.
Consequence 2 · a severity distinction the word “model” hides A stimulus model that goes stale mis-steers: you get worse coverage per run, and the coverage report tells you. A generated checker that goes stale is a bad oracle — it decides whether an observed response is correct — and a bad oracle does not announce itself in any report.

SayThat second box is the hinge of the whole chapter. Everything from here on is about generated checkers, because they are the artifacts whose failure is silent.

Chapter 25 · Language models, with the evidence

A framework built for one artifact class does not transfer to another

A 2025 study attacks a different target: protocol formal specifications — documents with timing diagrams. Its flow is two-stage: generate candidate properties from a grammar, check each against the timing diagram with a SAT solver, then use a language model to filter the survivors against the textual description. The SAT stage retains between 5% and 22% of the candidates in every case — the clearest available measure of how aggressively a mechanical filter can thin a candidate set before anyone reads it. Across six open-source on-chip protocols, the extracted properties covered 53 of 58 manually derived target properties — for every protocol but one, which is excluded from that count.

The head-to-head, and the reason it is not a curve That paper also runs the specification-driven framework of the previous slide on one of its own protocols. The two results must not be read as a curve. They differ in task and in artifact class, and the head-to-head re-ran that framework itself, reporting it under two underlying models. On that protocol the two-stage flow covered all 13 manually derived properties with no syntax errors, while under one of those two models the specification-driven framework produced 38 properties of which 3 were judged correct and 2 of the 13 targets covered, with 16 syntax errors and 11 references to signals that do not exist. The conclusion is not that one framework is better than another by some ratio — and the eleven non-existent signals track the model rather than the framework: under the other model it produces none.
A taxonomy worth borrowing wholesale — it is the vocabulary a reviewer needs A generated property may be correct (unique or redundant), incorrect, unsure, or hallucinated — invented rather than drawn from the set it was given to filter. One protocol's transaction section produced four hallucinated properties.
The authors' own limits, stated plainly The method depends on unambiguous timing diagrams. On the complex protocols the target set does not cover the entire formal specification. Models remain poor at reading figures, often getting a signal's value in a given cycle wrong. And experienced engineers are still better at inferring properties where the document has no text to work from.

Chapter 25 · Language models, with the evidence

“Around 40%” — and then read what was counted

The corpus contains one industrial report of an end-to-end agentic formal flow, from Infineon in 2025: a lead agent drafts a verification plan in natural language, a dispatcher delegates assertion generation, critic agents evaluate the results, properties are proven in a commercial model checker, counterexamples are analysed, and formal coverage is assessed for sign-off. Its headline is that the flow does not guarantee a result on every run and works most of the time, at an overall efficacy of around 40%.

What the 40% measures The study's first-named indicator is a success rate, defined as the number of runs that completed end-to-end formal verification out of the total runs — a measure of whether the pipeline finished, not of whether the properties it produced were the right ones.

On this flow those are different questions. Chapter 25 · Why a wrong one is expensive

Correctness is not the objective function. Plausibility is.

// Specification: "While the engine is holding a request
// that the memory path has not accepted, the key slot
// selector must not change."
// GENERATED -- and wrong, in two directions.
property p_keysel_stable_gen;
  @(posedge clk) disable iff (!rst_n)
  (req_valid && !req_ready) |-> $stable(key_sel);
endproperty
a_keysel_stable_gen : assert property (p_keysel_stable_gen);
// CORRECT -- the obligation lands one tick later.
property p_keysel_stable;
  @(posedge clk) disable iff (!rst_n)
  (req_valid && !req_ready) |-> ##1 $stable(key_sel);
endproperty
a_keysel_stable : assert property (p_keysel_stable);
c_keysel_held   : cover property (@(posedge clk)
                    disable iff (!rst_n)
                    (req_valid && !req_ready)[*2]);

The signals are right, the guard is present, the condition is the one the sentence describes, and the operator is the one stable suggests. It is wrong in two directions, from one missing delay.

1 · it forbids something legal $stable compares the current tick against the previous one, so at the first held cycle the property demands that key_sel already had its value one cycle earlier — before the request existed. A design that legally presents a fresh request with a new key slot right after a previous transfer fails a rule the specification never stated.
2 · it permits the damage On the last held cycle it says nothing about key_sel in the following cycle — the one in which the request is finally accepted, where a change would do actual damage.

Now ask what a formal proof would say about the first version. It depends entirely on whether this pipeline happens to hold key_sel steady for a cycle before a request rises. If a register upstream loads the selector one cycle early, the wrong property is proven on RTL that is behaving correctly — and under a pass criterion of proved against known-good RTL it is scored correct and enters the suite. A proof engine's verdict on today's RTL is not a verdict about the property.

SayThe defect is not invented: a 2025 study of property generation from protocol documents opens with it on a generic valid/ready interface, and notes that earlier machine-learning work made exactly the same mistake.

A HOLISTIC GUIDE30

Chapter 25 · Review practice

A generator's mistakes are systematic — so sampling a batch estimates nothing

PRACTITIONER JUDGMENT  A human writing forty properties makes idiosyncratic mistakes, uncorrelated across the batch, so sampling five is defensible: the sample estimates a rate. A generator's mistakes are systematic — one misreading of one recurring pattern reproduces identically in every property derived from it, so a sample of five either catches the whole class or misses it entirely. The chapter offers this as its own judgment rather than a published result.

What this review checks that an ordinary code review need notWhy it carries weight here that it does not carry when the author is a person
Every referenced signal exists and is the one intendedGenerated properties can name signals not in the design — the head-to-head counted eleven in a batch of thirty-eight. Elaboration catches those. It does not catch the wrong-but-existing signal, the common case where ports are similarly named.
The artifact is in a position to failVacuous passes are a named, observed failure mode in industrial use — which promotes Chapter 11's cover-with-every-implication rule from good habit to admission criterion.
The batch, not the sampleBecause a generator's errors are correlated, a review covers every artifact in a batch or rejects the batch.
Which sets the real constraint on adoption — and it inverts the intuition the technology invites A verification suite has collective authority: engineers believe a passing regression because they believe its checkers were written by someone who understood the design. One careful-looking property encoding the wrong rule spends that credit, and when the reviewer's scepticism runs out the outcome is not that generated properties get rejected — it is that all properties get skimmed. So the limit on throughput is not how fast artifacts can be generated but how fast they can be read: a flow producing two hundred properties in an afternoon has saved nobody a week unless someone reviews two hundred. A design constraint on the flow, not an attitude toward the technology.

Chapter 25 · Governance

Two years after tape-out, somebody asks where that property came from

A field failure triggers an escape analysis. Somebody asks how a behaviour was covered; the answer points at a property; the next question is where it came from. If the honest answer is a model wrote it and someone approved it, the review has nowhere to go.

Attach to every generated artifact — in the repository, not in a wiki
What produced itSystem, version, and the identity and version of the model underneath. One flow varied from 4.33% to 41.43% end-to-end success on a single tier depending on which. Model identity is a parameter of the result.
What it was givenWhich document, which revision, which section, and the prompt version. Without the revision, nobody can tell whether the property predates the change that made it wrong.
Sampling settings, and how many runs were keptSampling parameters are not a seed in Chapter 9's sense: identical inputs can yield different outputs across runs. And regeneration is not re-checking — a second run agreeing with the first is not corroboration, since both come from the same process with the same blind spots.
Which mechanical checks it passedAnd their versions: compilation, signal resolution, antecedent reachability, duplicate detection.
Who accepted it at each gate, and against what textNot a signature on a batch: a name against an artifact, and the specification sentence they compared it to.
Whether it was edited after generation, and by whomA hand-edited generated property is a hand-written property with a misleading history.
The last two are the ones teams skip and the ones a defensible sign-off needs A record of what a machine produced is an audit trail of the machine. A record of what a person accepted is an audit trail of the decision — and the decision is what is being reviewed.
A HOLISTIC GUIDE33

Chapter 25 · Deciding where to adopt

Three questions, fifteen minutes, and a decision you can defend

1. A check on the artifact that is independent of what produced it? A proof passed against the very design the artifact came from is not a check.

2. A baseline you already own and can re-measure on your own data? A vendor benchmark is not a baseline; your own last quarter is.

3. Does acceptance need a judgment about intent that no run can settle? Then the flow's throughput is that judgment's throughput.

ADOPT NOWWhere the first two hold and the third does not. Regression ranking and selection — the baseline is your own runtime, the check your own coverage database — with slide 23's constraint in the same breath. Supervised triage classification where a labelled failure history already exists, with a published agreement rate re-measured on a schedule. Generated scaffolding wherever a mechanical diff against a machine-readable source decides correctness.
PILOTIn a non-gating tier. Property generation from a specification: on a block whose specification is unusually precise, with reviewing capacity budgeted first, and with a statistic of your own — what fraction survived the intent gate — from the first batch. Counterexample debug assistance is the other good pilot: its output is a hypothesis a human was going to form anyway, and its fix can be applied and the proof re-run, a check the assistant does not control.
WAITOn flows whose sign-off artifact is the flow's own report. As of a 2026 survey, reinforcement-learning systems have reached industrial tape-out, and no language-model-driven autonomous agent had yet demonstrated a complete, human-free tape-out of an industrial chip. The survey also names the reading error to avoid — the unit-test fallacy, reading module-level pass rates as evidence about system-level capability. Every headline figure here was measured on a block, a protocol or a curated benchmark. None on an SoC.

SayOne governance failure cannot be corrected afterwards. A 2023 survey put the industrial concerns under three headings — model generalisation, model scalability and data governance — and spelled the third out: an engineer who cannot send her data outside her organisation, and has no machine-learning infrastructure inside it, cannot use a model that requires training on her data. So before a pilot starts, establish what leaves the building — specification, RTL, failure logs, coverage database — in writing, from whoever owns the design's confidentiality rather than from the team running the pilot.

Chapter 26 · A generation later

Everything about the programme improved. The bug got harder to find.

The flagship SoC's eight-channel DMA goes to tape-out: 40-bit physical addressing, descriptor chaining, the same frontend/midend/backend split and the same 4 KB legalization discipline inherited from the reference SoC. The verification programme around it is unrecognisable. The 4 KB rule exists as a formal property rather than only as a scoreboard check hoping to meet the right descriptor. The stimulus is a scenario space retargeted from simulation to the prototype instead of a constraint block written for one environment. Regressions gate on every commit, coverage merges across four platforms, and machine time is no longer what this block's team argues about.

Week 31 · SOC-1284's descendant The midend mis-legalizes a negative-stride row across a 4 KB frontier again — a different module, wider addresses, the same arithmetic mistake — and this time the second half of the split writes a line the host cluster holds Modified. The scoreboard that caught the original sees nothing, because the corrupted bytes never reach memory during the check; they surface at a writeback, later, somewhere else. The team finds it eventually, from a firmware symptom on the prototype: weeks of debug on a platform where the corrupted line is not visible, for a bug whose ancestor a scoreboard caught in one night.
What decided the outcome in 1284 Two hundred and fourteen directed tests passed honestly, and a constrained-random run stumbled into the combination. Nobody wrote the row saying that a data-integrity check crossing into a coherent domain is a different claim needing different evidence.

A HOLISTIC GUIDE38

Chapter 26 · Both ends of the flow

Each buys exactly one thing the other cannot

Shift-left means verification starting earlier, against something other than finished RTL. Clock-domain-crossing tools identify domain issues directly on an RTL model at earlier stages, because the metastability bugs they target cannot be demonstrated on an RTL model in simulation at all. Virtual platforms let firmware run against functional models before the hardware exists, and are sometimes the only option available.

What it genuinely buys is not earlier bug-finding: it is earlier specification finding. The most valuable thing that happens when you write an assertion against a document rather than a design is that the document turns out not to say what everyone assumed.

Its limit — the sensor hub Its always-on 32 kHz island, wake sources and retention list are architectural decisions, made and reviewed long before the RTL. Real work, done early, and not simulation of anything. What it cannot reach is the reference SoC's event-unit escape — a retention register losing its configuration when a wake event races the release of isolation — which surfaced in power-aware gate-level simulation after RTL sign-off. Shift-left moves the claims earlier. It does not move the evidence with them.

Shift-right means the design continues to be checked after tape-out. Checking real hardware rather than a model is not a fallback for what verification missed: it is a planned stage with its own instruments, and the industry pays for it in silicon — in some modern SoCs over 20 percent of the design real estate goes to silicon validation. What it buys is the one thing no pre-silicon platform has: the real device, at real speed, running real software for real durations.

What it cannot buy — and the constraint is arithmetic, not cultural Observability. Every mechanism writes into a fixed store, so signals × cycles ≤ buffer: the flagship's 64 KB trace buffer buys 128 traced signals for 4,096 cycles, or 256 for 2,048 — and the rectangle is fixed at tape-out. Which is why the deliverable of a post-silicon debug is not an explanation but a pre-silicon reproducer. Shift-right, done properly, is a machine for moving problems back into the middle of the flow.

SayTwo more shift-left instruments belong here: portable stimulus, which describes a scenario space once and retargets it; and continuous integration — one 2026 research-programme account reports that running UVM-based verification and FPGA-based checks inside CI increased productivity by making the verification flow transparent from RTL development.

JUDGMENT, NOT A FINDING  Both movements are correct in their own terms. The failure mode of doing both enthusiastically is a project with an excellent early plan, an excellent lab, and a thin layer of actual verification between them — the layer where evidence is cheapest to produce and easiest to trust.

Scenario · the flagship proposes halving the crossbar's block-level regression The argument offered has two halves: its protocol properties are formally proven, and its integration behaviour will be covered in the lab. Approach. The verification lead accepts the first and refuses the second. The proven properties genuinely do retire the protocol rows, so those runs no longer earn their place and go. The rows they do not retire are the ones about arbitration under sustained back-pressure across two managers — proven only up to a bound, and reachable in the lab but not diagnosable there inside the trace buffer. Those seeds stay.

Why. The two ends of the flow retired one class of evidence, and neither of them retired the other. Cutting by argument row by row is a different act from cutting by percentage — and only the first one can tell you which half you kept.

SayThe symptom to watch for in a team, not a plan: strong architectural modelling, a well-equipped lab, and block environments staffed by whoever is free. The middle is where both other skills are eventually cashed in — a pre-silicon reproducer is useless to a team that cannot run one.

Chapter 26 · The durable list

Would this still be true if every tool got a thousand times better?

Properties of the problem survive that test. Properties of the tooling do not — and a closing chapter that mixes them ages badly, because a reader ten years out will find half of it quaint and will not know which half. Six things pass.

1. The state space is exponential in the number of state bits, and abstraction is the only replyOne DMA channel's 192 bits of architecturally visible state make 2192 — about 1057 — configurations. A thousandfold faster engine buys you ten bits. The coherent hub makes it permanent: the MESI state of every line in every cache is a product space no tool will enumerate, so the proof runs against a model cut to two caches and one address. Not a workaround awaiting better capacity — it is the technique.
2. Simulation shows the presence of bugs, never their absenceA statement about sampling. No improvement in sampling rate converts a sample into a census.
3. Every practical oracle is partialEach checks a projection of correctness rather than correctness, and the craft is combining them so their blind spots do not overlap.
4. A proof is a statement about a model, under assumptions, sometimes to a depth“Formally verified” without those three qualifiers is not a claim. This is the durable form of “formal doesn't scale” — which is a tooling fact and will date. The qualifiers will not.
5. A plan is a set of claims, and coverage is a sample of a model you wroteRecording that a situation occurred and judging whether the design behaved correctly in it are two separate aspects of an environment: they draw on the same monitors, and their fidelities are traded off against one another. So “100 percent” is a statement about your model's own extent that says nothing about its fidelity.
6. Activation, propagation and checking are three independent conditionsA bug found requires all three, and each can fail alone; controllability and observability bound the first two. Which is why observability is designed in a year before it is needed, and why it is a rectangle rather than a dial.

SayItem 5 carries into Chapter 25 unchanged: a drafted plan is still a set of claims somebody's judgment shaped, and it still cannot see what it cannot represent.

Chapter 26 · The discipline

A profession, on four independent readings

HeadcountDemand for verification engineers has outgrown demand for design engineers since at least 2007 — a 12.5 percent compound annual rate against 3.7 percent through 2014, by which point the mean peak number of verification engineers on a project already exceeded the design count. The growth rates have since converged, but from that inverted baseline — and in some market segments, processors among them, a five-to-one ratio is not unusual.
BoundaryNo longer organisational. In 2024, design engineers spent on average 49 percent of their time on verification activities. The profession is defined by the activity rather than by the org chart.
ApparatusIts own standards: an assertion language, a testbench methodology, a stimulus description language, a coverage interchange format, a power-intent format — and, as of March 2026, a standard for describing an IP's clock- and reset-domain-crossing interface so that different teams' tools can exchange collateral about it. What a field standardises tells you where its work actually is: that list is not about running simulations faster, it is about making claims portable between people.
TheoryState-space arguments, the oracle problem, coverage as sampling, proofs qualified by model and assumption and depth — a body of reasoning that is genuinely its own, and that a good verification engineer uses daily whether or not they can name it.
The honest pitch, for someone choosing it Narrow and strong: the product is judgment — and judgment is what the tooling has been least able to absorb through four generations of trying. The work is intellectually severe in a specific way: you spend your time reasoning about what could be true rather than about what is. It also carries an unusual professional obligation, independence of judgment, which comes down to a willingness to say “no, this is not the way to do it” to a designer with more years on the block, or to a schedule that wants the block closed. A duty rather than a personality trait — and the day it is inconvenient is the day it counts.

Chapter 26 · The discipline

… and four things it still handles badly

JUDGMENT, NOT PESSIMISM  Every one of the four is a place where an individual can be visibly better than the average.

It does not learn from its escapesEscape analysis, where it happens at all, is usually a post-mortem slide rather than a change to the next plan and the next sign-off checklist. The mechanism for compounding organisational knowledge exists, is cheap, and is skipped.
It cannot estimateDebug takes more time than anything else, is unpredictable, and varies significantly between projects — which makes planning a project's effort from the previous project's data a stated management problem, not a solved one. In practice, teams estimate that way regardless. Three quarters of IC/ASIC projects were behind their original schedule in 2024, against a historical average of about two thirds.
It rarely measures its own environmentsEvery instrument in this book measures the design. Mutation is the one technique that answers “would this check have caught a change?” — and in practice it runs on a small fraction of blocks. A checker nobody has seen fail is a decoration, and that is true at the scale of a whole environment, not just of a single assertion.
It has no common language for “how verified is this?”A functional sign-off aggregates coverage and plan rows. A safety argument aggregates fault classification against per-level metric thresholds. A security result is a list of named claims and named gaps with no percentage available at all, and there will not be one soon. The three are not commensurable, they arrive at the same tape-out review, and the discipline has no accepted way to state a combined position. An open problem, not a solved one presented badly.

SayThe cheap countermeasure to the first item and the fourth: keep an explicit register of what the current programme is not verifying — the gaps, not just the waivers — because a named gap is reviewable and an unnamed one is a surprise. It has no metric attached, which is why it loses its budget argument to things that do.

Part VII · One idea to carry out of twenty-six chapters

Verification does not produce a correct design. It produces a true sentence about one.

What it produces is this: this is what we established, by this method, under these assumptions, and here is what we did not establish. Sign-off is the moment that sentence is spoken aloud and somebody's name goes under it.

Everything in this book is machinery for making that sentence mean more
A planthe sentence written down in advance
Coverageevidence that the sentence's scope was actually visited
A scoreboardthe part that could have made it false
An assertiona clause of it, checked continuously
A waiverthe sentence being honest about its own edges
A proofa clause with no exceptions inside a named model
An escape analysisthe sentence being repaired after it turned out to be too generous
SOC-1284 is what a false sentence looks like from the inside Two hundred and fourteen directed tests passed, honestly. The dashboard was green. The coverage report was accurate about everything it measured. The sentence all of that supported was the DMA's 2D legalization is verified — which was not true, and no instrument in the project could say so.

The question to bring to every review, every plan and every green regression for the rest of your career is not are we done? It is: what exactly are we claiming, and what would have to be true for that claim to be false?

If you can answer the second half, you are verifying. If you cannot, you are counting.
