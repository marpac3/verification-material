Acceleration and Emulation

Compile the design into hardware and it runs a hundred to a thousand times faster. Everything else about the way you work has to change to pay for it.

A HOLISTIC GUIDE4

Chapter 17 · Why simulation runs out

Boot the operating system before tape-out. Now price it.

Simulation with RTL models is approximately a billion times slower than the target clock of the system it models. Map the same RTL onto a fabric or a purpose-built emulator and it runs about 100–1000× faster than the simulator.

The same activity, two platformsWhat the source reports
An operating-system boot — seconds on target silicon — on an RTL simulatorseveral years
The same boot on an accelerated platformback inside a few hours
Do not divide these They are two independent anchors quoted from one source, not a ratio and its quotient. Divide "several years" by the 100–1000× band and you manufacture a third figure that composes with neither. Quote both; the argument turns on years vs hours, and no reordering of the numbers moves that boot into a nightly window.

SayRead all of these as order-of-magnitude claims — the band spans a factor of ten and where you land depends on design size, how much is instantiated, and how much instrumentation you compiled in.

A HOLISTIC GUIDE5

Chapter 17 · Why simulation runs out

The gap is structural, and buying machines does not close it

Why the ratio exists

An event-driven simulator turns the design's parallelism into its own serialism: every signal change is a software event, and every process sensitive to it is re-evaluated in sequence. A million gates switching in one design cycle are a million pieces of work for one processor.

Why it stopped improving

For two decades the industry rode processor frequency. Frequency scaling plateaued, and simulation stopped keeping pace with design complexity. The obvious escape does not exist: an individual RTL simulation does not usefully exploit multiple cores.

The parallelism fallacy

"We will add machines." A farm's width multiplies runs; it does not shorten a run. Cycle n of a boot depends on cycle n−1, so one run has no parallel fraction to exploit.

The triage question Is my problem the number of runs, or the length of one run? Ask it out loud before any conversation about capital equipment. Only the second answer leads to Chapter 17.

And the arithmetic is per single execution. The flagship's nightly candidate list is 6,400 simulations totalling 4,409 CPU-hours — already re-tiered and pruned to fit a night. Accurate metastability injection degrades simulation a further 3–4×.

SayA 4,800-run random suite is a licence problem. One boot is a cycles problem. Same word "slow", two unrelated diseases.

Chapter 17 · Use models

In-circuit or virtual: the environment decides, not the feature list

In-circuit (ICE) — the environment is the world

The design runs at MHz; a real link runs at its specified rate and has timeouts. A speed adapter buffers, throttles and re-times between them — the quiet asterisk on every ICE fidelity claim: real content at manufactured timing.

buys stimulus nobody could write (a real driver stack, quirks and all); interoperability against third parties you have no model of; compliance procedures that exist only as instruments.

forecloses repeatability (no seed, nothing to replay — and Chapter 12's whole triage apparatus assumes a run is a function of recorded inputs); scheduling; unattended operation; save/restore, because you cannot rewind the world.

Virtual — the platform owns model time

Built on SCE‑MI, the platform generates clocks on the emulator and tracks model simulation time inside it. Own model time and you can stop it, resume it, replay it, and place an event at an exactly chosen instant.

Everything the industry wanted follows: deterministic reruns, controlled clock relationships, injection at a specified cycle, waveform display, and an elimination strategy — disable a subset of injection points, rerun the identical scenario, see if the failure persists.

The rule Use ICE when the environment is the thing you cannot model; virtual for everything else. A compliance instrument, a third-party host, a driver stack you do not own — those are environments. Noise, traffic patterns and workloads are data: recordable once, replayable forever, and paying the irreproducibility tax for nothing.

SayThe USB compliance suite closes the protocol-state half of its vplan rows. Timing at the pins belongs to the adapter, not the design — those rows stay open until silicon.

A HOLISTIC GUIDE10

Chapter 17 · The transactor boundary

Where you draw it, not the headline frequency, sets your throughput

f = platform clock rate. N = design cycles advanced between host interactions. L = cost of one host interaction, in equivalent design cycles. Each period delivers N cycles and costs N + L:

R(N) = f · N / (N + L)
S(N) = R(N)/R(1) = N (1 + L) / (N + L)

S saturates at 1 + L however large N grows — batching can never buy more than the round-trip cost itself — and reaches exactly half that ceiling at N = L.

The design rule Not "use transactions". Make transaction granularity comparable to your round-trip cost — and stop optimising once it is.

Fraction of the machine's peak rate you actually get, N/(N + L). L is the parameter you least control — take it as tens to thousands of cycles:

Cycles per host interactionL=20L=200L=2000
1 (pin-level)4.8%0.5%0.05%
16 (a short burst)44%7.4%0.8%
256 (a max-length burst)93%56%11%

Pin-level throws away between 95% and 99.95% of what you paid for. And the rightmost column is the warning against complacency: on a costly link even a full 256-beat burst leaves you at a tenth of peak. If your numbers look like that column, the answer is a coarser transaction — a descriptor, a page, a frame — not a faster link.

SayThis table is fraction of peak rate. It is not S(N); the speed-up and the efficiency are different quantities with different ceilings.

Chapter 17 · The transactor boundary

The mechanical change is small, local, and entirely about who owns the clock

// Below the boundary: synthesised onto the platform.
module wr_xactor #(parameter int ADDR_W = 40, parameter int DATA_W = 512)
  (input  logic clk, rst_n, awready, wready,
   output logic [ADDR_W-1:0] awaddr, output logic [7:0] awlen,
   output logic [DATA_W-1:0] wdata,
   output logic awvalid, wvalid, wlast);
  logic [31:0] lfsr;
  export "DPI-C" task do_write_burst;
  task do_write_burst(input longint unsigned addr,
                      input byte unsigned len, input int unsigned seed);
    lfsr = seed;  @(posedge clk);  while (!rst_n) @(posedge clk);
    awaddr <= ADDR_W'(addr); awlen <= len; awvalid <= 1'b1;
    do @(posedge clk); while (!awready);
    awvalid <= 1'b0;
    for (int i = 0; i <= int'(len); i++) begin
      lfsr   = {lfsr[30:0], lfsr[31] ^ lfsr[21] ^ lfsr[1] ^ lfsr[0]};
      wdata  <= {(DATA_W/32){lfsr}};
      wlast  <= (i == int'(len)); wvalid <= 1'b1;
      do @(posedge clk); while (!wready);
    end
    wvalid <= 1'b0; wlast <= 1'b0;
  endtask
endmodule

Four load-bearing things

  • The task is timed; the call is not. Everything waiting on clk is inside the module. Do not use clocks to synchronise with the testbench — a driver that waits for an edge has re-created the pin-level case by another route.
  • One call covers len+1 beats. At awlen's maximum of 255, that is the N = 256 row of the table for one host interaction.
  • One source serves both engines. A simulator executes this directly, so a bug found on either platform reproduces on the other — as long as the two remain one source.
  • The payload is manufactured on the platform. The host passes a 32-bit seed; the shift register makes the data. Shipping a 512-bit beat 256 times is 256 interactions again.

SayAbove the boundary an interface imports the same task and the driver becomes one call. Anything the environment needs every cycle becomes hardware — that sentence predicts how much of a testbench has to be rewritten.

Chapter 17 · Economics

A licence pool degrades gracefully under contention. An emulator is granted, or it is not.

N runs cost N·s/Wsim on the farm and B + N·r/Whw on hardware. The forgotten term is Whw: hardware parallel width is capacity divided by design size, so a full-SoC image gives Whw = 1.

Flagship workloadThe arithmetic
OS bootN a handful per revision; s years; r a few hours; B hours to days. Break-even below one run: it does not need a business case, it needs a slot.
DMA legalization suite4,800 runs, 30-minute mean, 2,400 CPU-hours, ten hours of wall clock on 240 licences. Even at r < 2 s, Whw = 1 gives about 2.4 hours of machine time — then add B, paid per design revision. A nightly suite runs per revision, so it buys nothing.
  1. No simulation answer: boot, firmware, long soaks, NoC statistics.
  2. Campaigns whose value is iteration count under one image, where the build amortises across the sweep.
  3. Reproductions of failures found downstream: the platform has the observability silicon lacks.
  4. Nothing a farm can do — the rule always broken.
One extra vplan column — not the obvious one An emulation row must name which image it runs on. The image is the scheduling unit, so rows batch by image, not priority. Plan the images first, then assign rows — or discover, late, that the top-priority ones need five builds you have no calendar for.

Chapter 18 · The opening

The bug was not hard. The nine days were.

Four FPGA boards in a rack. Nine months before tape-out the flagship boots an operating system, mounts a filesystem over Ethernet, reaches a shell — and works for about forty seconds. Then a compute-cluster benchmark returns a result whose last cache line is stale. Run it again: passes. Run it forty times: fails twice.

What nobody schedules. Verification cannot reproduce it — their DMA and coherence tests pass and the failing workload is hundreds of millions of cycles long. Software's driver has run six months against a functional model without a failure. Available evidence: one console, a few LEDs, and a trace buffer configured for a different question three bitstreams ago.
Nine days later The flagship's DMA mis-legalizing a 2D descriptor at a 4 KB boundary — the reference SoC's canonical bug, inherited, and hidden one layer deeper by coherence, because the split's second half writes a line the host cluster holds Modified and the corruption stays invisible until the writeback.
The terminological line this chapter turns on Validation means checking a design on real hardware rather than on a model — post-silicon on fabricated parts, or in the lab on a prototype. So an FPGA prototype is pre-silicon in time and validation in kind. Not a faster simulator: a different kind of evidence, with the lab's debug economics attached.

SayNine days of ownership argument, for a bug both teams already knew the shape of. Everything in this chapter is aimed at that number.

A HOLISTIC GUIDE15

Chapter 18 · A different instrument

Three properties, all doing work — and one collapse that pays for them

What the prototype is for

  • Hardware speed — megahertz: roughly 100–1000× an RTL simulator, turning an OS boot from an impossibility into a few hours of runtime.
  • Real I/O — the console is a real UART, the network a real PHY. The driver being exercised is the driver, not a testbench's idea of one.
  • Cycle accuracy — no abstraction sits between the software and the design it programs.
The shape of what that buys A reported 2026 flow runs an FPGA test suite of 1,738 tests in three classes — RTL-against-platform integration, bare-metal benchmark and litmus, and OS-level driver and stack tests. Inside it, a full Linux boot of a dual-core design clocked at 20 MHz completes in 115 seconds. Two minutes, on a design far smaller than the flagship, for a boot RTL simulation would never finish.

The defining limitation is not speed or capacity

It is that you cannot see inside it. Observability is restricted to a few thousand internal signals; you must decide which before the bitstream is generated; and changing your mind means recompiling — several hours. Compare the simulator you left behind, where every net is visible for free, after the fact, without rerunning anything.

So prototyping emphasises system-level execution and functional realism, and deep debug is performed selectively or deferred to a higher-visibility environment. Deferred is the word to notice: it means reproducing the failure in simulation or on an emulator — exactly what the opening scenario's teams could not do.

Turnaround compounds it. Synthesis and partitioning take many hours or days, and probes are logic, competing for the same fabric and routing as the design. The loop that costs seconds in simulation — add a probe, rerun, look — costs a working day here.

SayWhat you can see is decided before the run, and changing your mind is a compile. That one sentence explains every workflow difference in this chapter.

A HOLISTIC GUIDE16

Chapter 18 · Capacity and clocking

Two distortions the platform introduces, and the one thing it cannot host at all

Partitioning inserts machinery the design does not have

Bridges between partitions, serialiser/deserialiser links across the board, synchronisation logic to hold the pieces together — at the cost of communication latency across FPGA boundaries. A crossbar or NoC path costs materially more cycles across a partition boundary than inside one device.

State this before anyone quotes a number Performance measured on a partitioned prototype is a lower bound with an unknown gap, never a projection. The arbitration and back-pressure behaviour you observe is not the behaviour silicon will have.

Clocking distorts selectively — derive it

The system clock drops by some factor to close timing in fabric, and the peripheral domain drops with it, so the reference SoC's 4:1 ratio (200 MHz : 50 MHz) survives — divide both by the same factor and the relationship holds. The always-on domain does not cooperate: its 32 kHz reference is a real crystal on the board, and a crystal does not scale. The 6,250:1 ratio becomes 6,250 divided by the system slowdown factor.

The canonical wake-cause bug lives exactly there: a legal 3'b010 → 3'b100 transition briefly observed as the reserved code 3'b110. Whether the prototype shows it depends on how many fast-domain sampling edges fall inside the slow domain's transition window — the very quantity you just changed. A prototype that never reproduces a crossing bug and one that reproduces it constantly can be the same design at two clock settings.

Write it in the plan as an explicit prototype-scope exclusion The flagship's LPDDR4 controller and PHY — whose training state machine is among the riskiest things on the chip — are exactly what cannot be prototyped: the PHY is analog and its timing is the point. On the board they are replaced by the platform's own memory controller. The prototype will run the OS beautifully and tell you nothing whatever about memory training.

SayStructural CDC analysis found the crossing bug in seconds. The prototype is not the instrument for it — in either direction.

A HOLISTIC GUIDE17

Chapter 18 · What earns a place

Not "cheap emulator" — one instrument on each side of the verification/validation line

The right mental model An emulator is a fast simulator with reduced visibility. A prototype is early silicon with more visibility than silicon.
Work you would do in a simulator, if only it were faster, belongs on an emulator. Work you would do in the lab, if only you had a chip, belongs on the prototype.

The admission test — all three must hold

  1. It needs more cycles than simulation can deliver. If it fits in a simulation run, run it there, where debug is cheaper.
  2. It has an oracle that survives the visibility collapse. An offline bit-exact diff, self-checking firmware, an in-fabric monitor latching into a readable register. A workload whose only oracle was "the engineer watched the waveform" has no oracle at all here.
  3. Its failure is plausibly reproducible in a higher-visibility environment — because that is where it will be debugged.

Condition 2 is the one teams skip; condition 3 is the one that costs them.
Scenario · a workload that passes all three The video codec's encoder passes its block environment: bit-exact per macroblock, coverage closed on modes, quantiser steps, buffer-full. What nobody has run is a sequence — a thousand consecutive frames where rate control adapts across scene changes. Approach: run it on the prototype from a real clip, write the bitstream to storage, diff the whole output offline. Why it works: the cycles are genuinely needed; the oracle needs no visibility at all; and when frame 738 differs, the frame number and the encoder state are enough to build a simulation testcase. That last property is what makes it worth doing — not the speed.
The counter-case In the FPGA-target segment, where teams routinely minimise pre-lab verification to reach the lab quickly, only 13 percent of projects in the 2024 Wilson Research Group FPGA study reported zero non-trivial escapes into production. Prototypes make bugs visible late; they do not make them findable.

SayTrading a controllable, observable, reproducible environment for an uncontrollable, unobservable, weakly reproducible one — and gaining only speed. The same 2024 study pair puts IC/ASIC first-silicon success at 14%.

WRG / Siemens EDA 2024 trend reports — FPGA [R1, p.5]; IC/ASIC [R2, p.5]. Double-blind, ±4% at 95%.

Chapter 18 · Virtual platforms

Fast, indispensable, and structurally blind to one entire class of bug

establishes that the initialisation sequence sets the registers it means to, in an order the hardware accepts; that firmware's view of the memory map agrees with the architecture's; that handlers are wired to the sources they think; that device tree, linker script and boot ROM hand-off agree. On the flagship: four cores of OS bring-up, a driver stack and a filesystem, none of it waiting for RTL.

absent no arbitration — nothing to arbitrate. No back-pressure — a model that returns immediately never stalls. No latency — two operations the design will overlap are sequential. No coherence — a flat memory has nothing to be coherent with.

Absent, not undetected — which is worse The entire class of bugs that consists of two things happening at once is missing, and the platform reports success with complete confidence.

Two traps, both producing firmware that passes and then fails

  • Assumptions the model never charged for. A poll loop terminating on the first read against a model that completes instantly — so nobody notices it has no timeout and no memory barrier.
  • Synchronisation the model made unnecessary. A buffer handed to DMA without cache maintenance works perfectly with no caches. It surfaces on the prototype looking exactly like a hardware coherence bug — and is argued about as one.
Scenario · the diagnostic signature A GPU driver, eight months green against a functional model, tears a frame once every few thousand submissions on the prototype. Do not start by suspecting the driver: ask which of its assumptions the model was structurally unable to test. The model had no concurrency to get wrong, so it could neither confirm nor deny the ordering between the command processor's descriptor read and the shader clusters' writes. It never posed the question. When a prototype failure looks like a race, ask first what the software's development platform could not model.

SayA third trap: the model becomes the specification by default — neither it nor the RTL is golden. And the approximately timed middle setting is not silent about timing: it has a timing, plausible, quotable, and not the design's. It answers architecture questions, whose answer is a ratio; not verification questions, whose answer is a verdict.

Chapter 18 · Hybrid co-simulation

Where you put the boundary is the experiment

Virtual platform on the hostfast instruction-set models: what you are NOT verifying
TLM channeluntimed hand-off
Engine executing selected RTLcycle accurate: what you ARE verifying

The placement rule — three things true at once

  1. Already an architectural boundary with transaction semantics. A register interface, a bus port, a mailbox. A boundary drawn mid-microarchitecture — across a pipeline, a credit counter, a handshake — has no transaction to send.
  2. The virtual side is mature. You are trusting it, unverified, for the duration of every run. A model written last week to fill a hole in the partition is a second design with no test plan.
  3. Everything whose timing you care about is on the RTL side.
What the boundary deletes The channel is untimed, so every bug living in the timing relationship between the two sides is unreachable by construction. If the core and the DMA contend for one memory port and only the DMA is RTL, the contention is gone — not modelled coarsely, gone. That is not a defect in hybrid platforms; it is what they are. It is a defect only when nobody writes it down.

So write it down, on the plan row: "cross-boundary timing between the host cluster and the compute island is out of scope on this platform; covered by block-level full-RTL simulation and by the prototype's system regression." A written exclusion is a coverage decision; an unwritten one is a hole with a green dashboard over it.

SaySame problem, two scales, opposite answers: on the reference SoC full-RTL simulation of the whole chip is affordable and a hybrid channel buys nothing; on the flagship it is how the driver team runs real code against the real compute cluster in month four rather than month eleven.

A HOLISTIC GUIDE20

Chapter 18 · Firmware-driven verification

Not a sequence that resembles what software will do — the binary that will ship

Constrained random asks is every legal transaction handled correctly? Firmware asks does this sequence, in this order, from this reset state, achieve the effect the software needs? — and answers for one trajectory. Orthogonal projections, and mainstream: in 2024, 84 percent of IC/ASIC projects incorporated one or more embedded processors.

  • Initialisation ordering. Access constraints and preconditions are frequently not documented, and required nonetheless. The driver's sequence is what silicon sees for the rest of its life.
  • Register access races. Three driver variants checked against each other: a missing side-effect annotation introduced a race that could make the device raise an interrupt incorrectly — and it had passed the simulation-based verification previously applied.
  • Error-recovery paths. Timeouts, retries, channel resets. Nobody writes directed tests for these; running the driver runs them anyway.
  • Integration wiring. The interrupt on the wrong input; the address the header and the decode logic disagree about. Block environments passed, the system regression passed, and it surfaced in the lab as a hang.
Two limits — the second is easy to miss Firmware is one path. It uses the burst lengths the driver chooses and the alignments the allocator produces. It will never issue the legal transaction the driver has no reason to issue, and never varies anything — a thin deep thread through a wide space. And firmware is not an oracle for the hardware. A driver checks that it achieved its own goal, not that the design behaved correctly. A hardware bug the retry logic silently absorbs is a passing boot with a defect in it.

SayThe crypto key ladder: once per few hundred re-keys a session decrypts to garbage, because the driver writes the next key-slot register while the previous operation is still draining — a requirement on the software, discovered in the hardware, written down in neither. Firmware-only ships a design that works for the driver it grew up with; random-only ships one where every transaction is legal and the initialisation sequence deadlocks.

// The same expression, in fabric. first_len/first_size dropped whole.
module dma_4kb_monitor #(parameter int unsigned ADDR_W = 40) (
  input  logic clk, rst_n, awvalid, awready, input logic [7:0] awlen,
  input  logic [ADDR_W-1:0] awaddr, input logic [2:0] awsize,
  input  logic [1:0] awburst, output logic viol_seen,
  output logic [15:0] viol_count, output logic [ADDR_W-1:0] first_addr);
  logic viol;
  always_comb viol = awvalid && awready && (awburst == 2'b01) &&
    ((16'(awaddr[11:0]) + ((16'(awlen) + 16'd1) << awsize)) > 16'd4096);
  always_ff @(posedge clk or negedge rst_n)
    if (!rst_n) begin
      viol_seen <= 1'b0; viol_count <= '0; first_addr <= '0;
    end else if (viol) begin
      if (!viol_seen) begin viol_seen <= 1'b1; first_addr <= awaddr; end
      if (viol_count != 16'hFFFF) viol_count <= viol_count + 1'b1;
    end
endmodule

Concurrent assertions are a simulation and proof construct. There is no assertion engine in the fabric. The check must become logic that reports its verdict through a register the driver can read.

What it costs

  • The waveform. One address, one length, one size — and no picture of what the interconnect was doing around them.
  • Every violation after the first — hence the counter. It separates systematically illegal descriptors from one corner case fired once.
  • Fabric. Real logic, competing for the same routing as the design — the currency that buys debug probes.
What it keeps Evaluated at the instant of the violation, at full speed, on every burst of a workload no simulator will reach. Detection distance: zero cycles — though the distance to your knowledge of it is however long until the driver next polls.

Chapter 19 · The silent cousin

An environment that does not fail — it quietly stops checking

// `!==` is case inequality: its result is always 1'b0 or
// 1'b1, so an unknown in `act` always reaches the report.
// `!=` is logical inequality -- 1'bx when the relation is
// ambiguous -- and `if (1'bx)` takes the false branch,
// scoring the transaction as correct.
always_comb begin
  if ($isunknown(act))
    $error("unknown in sampled data: %0b", act);
  else if (act !== exp)
    $error("mismatch: got %0h, expected %0h", act, exp);
end

A 2-state variable holding that result would convert the x to 0 by a second route.

The language rule is exact, and the exactness matters. For the logical equality and inequality operators, the result is 1'bx only when unknown or high-impedance bits make the relation ambiguous. The case operators include those bits in the comparison and always yield a known result.

So != does not simply "miss unknowns". A value corrupted only in bits that already differ from the expectation is still reported, because the relation is not ambiguous. What escapes is the fully corrupted value, and the partially corrupted one whose unknowns sit precisely on the bits that would have carried the difference.

Why the defect survives review The checker works on most of the data most of the time, and fails exactly where the data is worst. It fails silently — the run is green, the coverage is collected, the plan row is discharged. Audit this before a testbench is ever pointed at a netlist. The cure is two lines: an explicit unknown check ahead of the comparison, so that "unknown" is its own verdict rather than a special case of "equal".

SayThis one is worth a slow read. Two hours of a nine-hour debug went into a checker that was reporting success.

Chapter 19 · SDF back-annotation

Three ways annotation lies quietly, and the artifact that catches each

RTL has no delays. A netlist has library delays — estimates for an average output load, while every instance of the same cell drives a different number of inputs over wires of different lengths. Back-annotation replaces them with per-instance values. What SystemVerilog takes from the file is precisely bounded: specify path delays, specparam values, timing-check constraint values, and interconnect delays. Everything else in an SDF file is ignored.

Chapter 19 · The notifier

A timing check prints. The notifier is what makes a violation functional.

// Abridged library flip-flop model. Rows handling unknown inputs
// are elided; in a real cell they outnumber these, and they are
// where gate-level pessimism comes from.
primitive dff_udp (q, clk, d, notifier);
  output q; reg q;  input clk, d, notifier;
  table
  // clk  d  ntf : state :  q
       r  0   ?  :   ?   :  0 ;
       r  1   ?  :   ?   :  1 ;
       f  ?   ?  :   ?   :  - ;
       ?  *   ?  :   ?   :  - ;
       ?  ?   *  :   ?   :  x ;  // notifier event => q goes x
  endtable
endprimitive
module dff_with_timing_check (output wire q, input wire clk,
                              input wire d);
  reg notifier;
  dff_udp u_ff (q, clk, d, notifier);
  specify
    specparam tSU = 120, tHLD = 40;  // SDF overwrites per instance
    $setuphold(posedge clk, d, tSU, tHLD, notifier);
    (posedge clk => (q +: d)) = 90;
  endspecify
endmodule

Every timing check may take a notifier — a variable the check toggles on violation, so the model can make its behaviour a function of violations. The canonical use: drive the output to x.

Why gate-level debug feels the way it does A timing violation is not a message you note and move past. It injects an unknown into the datapath, which propagates and produces a functional failure somewhere else entirely, thousands of cycles later. "Twelve timing violations, all in one block, early in the run" is never benign — it is the cause of whatever the scoreboard reports at the end. Resolve timing-check output first, in time order.

STA is the authority; GLS samples

But the constraints file STA reads is manually generated, and every exception in it — false path, multicycle path, case analysis — is a human claim STA obeys rather than checks. A back-annotated run makes no such assumption: not a check on the timing, but a check that the constraints described the design you actually built.

SayThe multicycle path declared correct at 8-bit weights is single-cycle at 2-bit weights. STA reports timing met, because it was told not to look. Run the configurations the constraints assumed something about.

Chapter 19 · Power-aware verification

Nobody wrote those checks. The corruption is the checking mechanism.

PD_AON — always onpower controller, wake logic, 32 kHz
isolationclamp to a chosen value
PD_SYS — switchedcorrupted to X while powered down; retention registers restored on exit

HDLs were built to capture what a design computes. They have no way to say how each element is powered — and the same RTL is instantiated into different power architectures by different integrators, so power intent must live outside the golden design description.

Five words, pinned precisely. A power domain is instances grouped for power management, sharing a supply set. Isolation gives defined behaviour for a signal whose driver is not active, via an isolation cell that clamps to a specified value. A level-shifter translates between voltage swings. Retention lets selected sequential elements survive power-down. A power state table states the legal combinations.
What loading power intent changes Every logic node in a powered-down domain is corrupted to unknown and stays unknown until power returns. An isolation cell stops that unknown reaching powered-on logic. A retention register is corrupted like everything else, except that the value saved before entry is restored on exit.

So a missing isolation cell is not diagnosed by a rule — it is diagnosed by the failure it now causes: the unknown appears on the crossing, and downstream logic starts misbehaving shortly after the driving domain goes down.

Simstate makes the level of corruption a declared property: NORMAL → CORRUPT_STATE_ON_CHANGE → CORRUPT_STATE_ON_ACTIVITY → CORRUPT_ON_CHANGE → CORRUPT_ON_ACTIVITY → CORRUPT. State ON carries NORMAL; OFF and ERROR both carry CORRUPT.

SayRead the simstate ladder as a question you are being made to answer about every state you designed: does logic still switch here, does it hold without switching, or can it not even hold?

A HOLISTIC GUIDE31

Chapter 19 · Sequencing

The ordering is not a convention. It is what the hardware requires.

Power-downPower-up
1. Quiesce: no outstanding bus transactions1. Assert the domain reset, while power is still off
2. Gate the domain clock2. Close the header switch; wait for supply-good
3. Assert save: retention captures state3. Release reset, then assert restore
4. Assert isolation: outputs clamp4. Release isolation
5. Open the header switch: power off5. Un-gate the clock
Three of these steps carry published failures Isolation must be asserted before sleep enable and released after it — swap either and the always-on island samples an unknown for the overlap. Reset must already be asserted when the supply returns: a design asserting reset after sleep-enable de-asserted showed the register output corrupted for exactly the window between supply arriving and reset landing. And reset must be inactive at the restore edge — which is why step 3 has an order inside it.

Where inspection stops working

2 states — reference SoC: one switchable domain. One power-down sequence, one power-up sequence, reviewable by three people around a whiteboard in an afternoon. The table is documentation; directed tests check the sequences.
128 combinations — flagship: six domains, DVFS giving the host cluster and the compute island four states each, the other three on or off. 4 × 4 × 2 × 2 × 2. An upper bound, not a count of legal states — but nobody reviews 128 by inspection, and nobody enumerates the transitions, which is where the sequencing bugs live. The table becomes machine-readable and the checks are generated from it.
4,000+ legal states — a published 2010 case at Samsung Electronics: 40 million gates, 30 power domains, eight software-controlled, more than 2,000 lines of power intent, power-state coverage named as a challenge with no satisfactory tooling answer. Coverage of the state space stops being achievable: prove the controller only emits legal sequences, and verify domains against those sequences.

Scale and the coverage challenge above; the three sequencing failures opposite: Low Power Verification with UPF, Samsung Electronics with Synopsys, DVCon 2010 — [D17, pp. 4, 5, 8]. Self-reported, and scale is all it measures — no before/after payoff, so nothing here says the checklist made anything faster. The two sequence columns are the reference SoC's, not Samsung's.

SayTwo, 128, four thousand: the same discipline, three regimes — and the technique has to change at each step.

Chapter 19 · The worked escape

The assertion that would have caught it needs no stimulus at all

// Bound in the always-on domain: every signal here is always-on, so the
// checker runs while the switched domain has no power. 2 of 4 shown.
module pd_sys_sequence_chk (
  input wire clk_aon, rst_aon_n,  // 32 kHz clock, async reset
  input wire iso_en,              // 1 = switched outputs clamped
  input wire sleep_en,            // 1 = the power switch is open
  input wire restore);            // retention restore, active high
  // Power may be removed only from an already-isolated domain.
  property p_iso_before_sleep;
    @(posedge clk_aon) disable iff (!rst_aon_n) $rose(sleep_en) |-> iso_en;
  endproperty
  a_iso_before_sleep : assert property (p_iso_before_sleep);
  // The clamp must hold for the whole restore window: the retention
  // strategy's restore-period condition, as an assertion.
  property p_restore_isolated;
    @(posedge clk_aon) disable iff (!rst_aon_n) restore |-> iso_en;
  endproperty
  a_restore_isolated : assert property (p_restore_isolated);
endmodule

The mechanism. The retention strategy carries a restore-period condition — "isolation still asserted" — and the standard is normative: it shall hold throughout the restore period, otherwise the restore fails and the retention element is corrupted. A fast-wake optimisation drops isolation the cycle a wake event arrives, once supply-good is asserted. The first wake cannot fire it: supply is still off. A second wake inside the restore window is qualified, so the bypass fires, the condition goes false mid-restore, and the interrupt mask is destroyed.

Plain RTL simulation cannot represent this; power-aware simulation can, but needs stimulus that hits the race. An assertion needs neither — it watches the controller's outputs, so it runs unchanged at RTL and is an excellent formal target.

Power-aware simulation has its own optimism Results reflect the implemented hardware only so far as the declared simstate is correct. Some mismatches are conservative; others are optimistic, producing errors in the implemented design that the simulation showed working.

SayRetention is a default to be argued against as well as for. Retain the crypto key registers and the engine works better — and the entire security value of powering the domain down is gone.

A HOLISTIC GUIDE35

Chapter 20 · The opening

Two silicon bring-ups, eleven months apart

Reference SoC — a part on a bench

One part, a JTAG probe, a scope on the 32 kHz crystal, a serial terminal. Thursday: powers on, releases reset, boots from ROM, blinks a GPIO. The following Friday: the DMA has moved a 2D tile and the UART echoes at 115,200 baud. Two engineers, six weeks. When something misbehaves they halt the core, read STATUS through the debug module — and, with 512 KB of SRAM and a handful of peripherals, usually guess right on the second try.

Flagship — the failure that costs three weeks

Under one mixture of decode and live network traffic, roughly once every forty minutes, the system stops. No exception, no error interrupt, no kernel log. The host cluster is idle, two NoC queues are full, the DMA reports no error on any channel, and every configuration register reads back exactly what firmware wrote.

The arithmetic that frames the whole chapter Forty minutes at target speed is of order 1012 cycles — 2,400 seconds at any point on the flagship's 0.6–1.5 GHz DVFS band, so every operating point lands on the same order. The on-chip trace buffer holds about four thousand of them.

What eventually closed it was not a debug technique. It was a decision taken fourteen months earlier, in a meeting nobody remembers, about which buses would get monitors, how deep the trace buffer would be, and whether those monitors would still be reachable when the compute island was power-gated.

SayThe uncomfortable centre of this chapter: the observability you have in the lab is the observability you designed in, months before you knew what would break.

Chapter 20 · The reordering claim

The goal of post-silicon debug is not to root-cause the bug

The goal is to narrow an observed failure down to an error scenario that can be investigated in a pre-silicon environment: a stimulus short enough to simulate, on a scope small enough to load, with the failure still present. Root-causing then happens where you can see.

Judge every technique in this chapter by how far it moves a failure toward that hand-off — not by how much it explains.

Scenario · the lab does not try to explain the corruption. It reduces. The video codec passes every pre-silicon check, and a customer's live stream produces one corrupted macroblock roughly every two hours of playback. Approach: capture the stream to memory; replay from DRAM instead of from the network; bisect to a 40-frame segment that still fails; then to a single frame; then hand the frame plus the decoder's register configuration to the pre-silicon team, who reproduce it in RTL simulation. Why: the oracle already exists and is golden — the bit-exact reference model runs on the captured stream on a workstation, so the lab can decide corrupt or not with no internal visibility at all. What the lab supplies is not a diagnosis but a reproducer, and each bisection step is worth more than any amount of staring at the trace buffer.

Chapter 20 · Design for debug

Two conflicts DfD must survive — both settled a year before the lab exists

Power management

Debug data has to travel, and its route runs through logic power management may switch off. A block can be perfectly instrumented and still invisible because the path carrying its signals crosses an IP that is powered down at the moment of interest — an IP with no functional relationship to the one under debug. The reflexive fix, disabling power management during debug, destroys the ability to debug the power sequences themselves. The architectural fix is the flagship's: one debug access port per power domain.

Scenario · the sensor hub's daily missed wake The DfD route from the always-on island to the trace buffer passes through the DSP island — power-gated precisely while the bug is armed. What makes the debug possible: a debug access port inside the always-on domain, plus a small always-on capture of the four signals that matter, sampled at 32 kHz. Observability of a domain must be powered by that domain. A trace path that depends on a neighbour's power state is observability you own only when you do not need it.

Security

DfD is, by construction, a back door into the part's internal state — and much of it stays present on-field so deployed systems can be diagnosed. That makes it an obvious path to the assets it sits beside: keys, firmware, debug modes. Published system compromises have used post-silicon observability features to get in. The answer is authenticated debug unlock through the root of trust, with the fabric's reach reduced once the part leaves the lab; the knee-jerk alternative, cutting DfD, buys security by making lab validation of the part impossible.

Both are resolved by people who will not be in the lab Post-silicon readiness — DfD architecture, debug software, debug boards, post-silicon test plans — is a pre-silicon workstream that starts at product-functionality planning and runs the whole length of the design cycle. Treat it like the verification plan: it has owners, rows and review gates, or it does not exist.

SayOn which signals to trace: there is no known industrial report of the state-restoration ratio ever being used to select them. Selection rests on experience — for each block, ask what failure you would most fear and what you would need to see to separate its two likeliest causes.

Chapter 20 · Triage and localisation

Detection is often free. Localisation is what costs the calendar.

The governing quantity is error detection latency: the time between a test activating a bug and that error becoming an observable failure. For difficult bugs it runs to millions or even billions of cycles.

Now put that next to the rectangle The run is of order 1012 cycles; the buffer at 128 signals wide holds 4,096. The trace covers roughly one part in 250 million of the execution — a figure deliberately conservative against the clock band. And placement is not free: even a perfect trigger capturing the last 4,096 cycles before the hang captures the symptom, while the activation sat millions of cycles earlier, outside the window by construction.

A checker tells you the answer is wrong. The trace buffer shows you four thousand cycles of a forty-minute film, and the interesting scene is not in them.

Bug resolutionwork around, group, localise, root-cause, prove the fix → a bug, resolved

The first classification question is one no pre-silicon triage rota ever asks: is this a silicon issue at all, or a logic error? A failure that follows the part is manufacturing or marginality; one that follows the design is yours.

The weakened standard, and why it is a good trade Because silicon is fast, an error appearing reliably once in a few executions still counts as reproducible. A one-in-five rate is a flake in a nightly regression; in the lab it is a working recipe, because five runs cost minutes.

Say"Cannot reproduce" almost never means non-deterministic — it usually means the recipe has a parameter nobody has varied. The radar's spurious detections appeared only above ~85 °C and only on parts calibrated in the lower third of the distribution: two conditions no bench at room temperature produces together.

Chapter 20 · Four moves

Four ways to attack a window that is 250 million times too small

1 · Bisect the configurationone run per question
 →
2 · Bisect in timemany forward runs, stitched
 →
3 · Shorten the latencysoftware-only transformation
 →
4 · Move to a platform that can seethe hand-off

1. Configuration bisection is cheap, coarse and criminally under-used. If the failure needs four cores, does it need three? One run per question — and it decides where the expensive instruments point. The GPU: clusters {0,1} fail; {0,2}, {1,3}, {2,3} do not; {0,1} with the DMA idle does not. Five runs reduce "somewhere in the GPU" to "two clusters sharing a port into unified memory, plus DMA traffic".

2. Cost the time bisection honestly Narrowing an order-1012 run to one 4,096-cycle window takes log2(1012/4,096) ≈ 28 halvings — at forty minutes a run, about nineteen hours of pure execution, and over two days if it reproduces one run in three. And the search is two-dimensional. A buffer twice as deep removes one halving out of twenty-eight. What collapses the cost is a trigger.

3. Attack the latency, not the search. Quick error detection shortens the interval between activation and observable failure — a read after each write catches a wrong value at once — and the transformations are software-only. Bounded model checking on top turns detection into localisation: on the OpenSPARC T2, a 500-million-transistor open-source multicore SoC, difficult logic bugs were localised automatically in under seven hours, with activating counter-examples up to six orders of magnitude shorter.

4. The formal hand-off, and why it is underrated Do not take the failing sub-system as the proof scope. Pick the smallest block boundary at which you can still observe the property; over-constrain everything that did not participate; leave the signals of interest under-constrained; load state from a waveform after initialisation. Reported at Intel: a post-silicon bridge failure reproduced and root-caused in a formal environment built from scratch in three person-days on a 25K-gate DUT, the fix validated in five person-days more, and the same environment reused on two later SoCs for eight further functional bugs.

SayThe plan row a silicon escape indicts is, by revealed preference, the row that deserves formal sign-off in the next generation.

Which question bites changes. Pre-silicon escapes are usually stimulus escapes. Post-silicon escapes concentrate in "why did the metrics say we were done?" — the dashboards were green. An escape analysis that cannot explain a green dashboard has not finished.
The codec escape record Why no stimulus? The corpus has streams with unusual slice geometry and streams with frequent reference-list updates — none with both: it is organised by feature.
Why were the metrics green? A coverpoint for slice geometry, one for reference-list update, both at 100%. No cross.
